# Finance Preference Tuning: Evidence, Returns, and Risks

## Executive findings

| Finding | Evidence and decision | Source URL |
|---|---|---|
| **Trading Evidence** | FinDPO is the clearest direct DPO-to-portfolio result located: **458.97% cumulative return**, **66.64% annualized return**, and **2.03 Sharpe at 5 bps** on a 417-stock S&P 500 universe over February 2015-June 2021. Exact day-level dates are NOT REPORTED; the comparison set is the paper's sentiment baselines, with the S&P 500 benchmark also reported. [8] [8] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 |
| **No Clean DPO-versus-SFT P&L Test** | FinDPO reports sentiment F1 comparisons with FinSFT, but the portfolio table does not report FinSFT. Therefore, the evidence does not establish that DPO beats SFT on trading P&L. [8] [8] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 |
| **RLSP Is Not Trading P&L Evidence** | FinGPT RLSP uses post-news stock-price movement as feedback and improves sentiment Macro-F1 from **77.3** after LoRA SFT to **80.9** after RLSP, but the paper reports sentiment classification rather than a transaction-cost-adjusted trading portfolio. [3] [3] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031 |
| **GRPO Finance Reasoning** | Fin-R1 uses SFT plus GRPO with format and answer-accuracy rewards, but its reported evaluations are financial reasoning and instruction datasets; stock-trading P&L is NOT REPORTED. [6] [6] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16252 |
| **RFT Trading Result** | Trading-R1 reports an RFT-style market-grounded reward and a held-out June 1-August 31, 2024 backtest. Its Table 3 reports **5.39% CR / 1.72 SR** for NVDA, **5.12% / 0.86** for AAPL, and **3.34% / 1.60** for MSFT; transaction costs are NOT REPORTED and the visible table does not provide paired baseline metrics. [7] [7] [7] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |
| **Synthetic Preferences Dominate the Sample** | FinDPO constructs chosen/rejected pairs from labels and reference-model outputs, not human preferences or realised-return preferences. LLMs Meet Finance similarly uses accepted database answers and rejected overlong SFT outputs. [8] [5] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
| **Market Rewards Are High-Risk** | FinGPT and Trading-R1 use market-linked signals, but FinGPT has no reported portfolio test and Trading-R1 does not state transaction-cost assumptions in the cited evidence. Treat positive backtest results as model-and-protocol-specific, not as evidence of deployable alpha. [3] [7] [7] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |
| **Reproducibility Is Uneven** | FinDPO reports **1 A100 40 GB for 4.5 hours**; LLMs Meet Finance reports **3-5 hours on 4 x 2080Ti** for each 1.5B model; GPU hours for FinGPT, Fin-R1, Trading-R1, and FinRankGRPO are NOT REPORTED. [8] [5] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
| **Honest Negative** | LLMs Meet Finance reports DPO gains on output length and one task but a decline in NER F1, and its RL gains are benchmark boosts with no trading-decision or P&L evaluation. [5] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |

The only fully specified direct preference-tuning portfolio evidence is FinDPO, and its portfolio table does not provide a clean DPO-versus-SFT comparison. [8] [7]

## 6. Failure modes and honest negatives

| Failure mode or negative | Evidence | Required metadata for any performance number | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frictionless backtest optimism** | FinDPO explicitly notes that frictionless evaluations can be overly optimistic and that high-turnover daily rebalancing is sensitive to transaction costs. [8] [8] | The cited limitation is qualitative; no additional performance number is introduced here. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 |
| **DPO can trade off task quality** | In LLMs Meet Finance, DPO reduces overlength and improves CC F1, but NER F1 falls from **0.6212** after SFT to **0.5950** after the further DPO stage. Universe: NER/CC tasks; dates: NOT REPORTED; costs: NOT REPORTED; baseline: SFT. [5] | Universe: NER/CC benchmark tasks; exact dates NOT REPORTED; costs NOT REPORTED; baseline SFT. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
| **Benchmark-only RL gains** | LLMs Meet Finance reports RL benchmark boosts but identifies no trading-decision experiment, realised-return score, numerical baseline, exact dates, or transaction costs for those boosts. [5] | Universe, dates, costs, and numerical baseline are NOT REPORTED. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
| **RLSP objective mismatch** | FinGPT's RLSP improves sentiment classification, but the cited paper does not report portfolio P&L, transaction-cost-adjusted returns, or an asset-level trading test. [3] | Any trading performance number: NOT REPORTED. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031 |
| **Trading-R1 cost disclosure gap** | Trading-R1 reports a held-out backtest and market-grounded reward, but the official table evidence does not specify transaction costs or provide the complete paired baseline metrics. [7] [7] [7] | For the reported CR/SR/HR/MDD: universe is Table 3's NVDA/AAPL/MSFT; window June 1-August 31, 2024; costs NOT REPORTED; paired baseline metrics NOT REPORTED. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |
| **No verified human-preference trading study located** | FinDPO uses synthetic label/reference-model pairs; Fin-R1 uses format and answer correctness; LLMs Meet Finance uses correctness and output-length pairs. Human preference labels are NOT REPORTED for these finance studies. [8] [6] [5] | No performance number introduced. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16252 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
| **Reward hacking and backtest overfitting** | No reviewed finance preference paper provides a quantified reward-hacking rate or a quantified backtest-overfitting estimate in the cited evidence. Trading-R1 states that the test period was held out and that decisions use information available up to each day, which is a control, not proof that overfitting is absent. [7] | No performance number introduced. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |

## 7. Synthesis: what the evidence supports

| Dimension | Market-outcome methods: FinGPT RLSP and Trading-R1 | Preference or verifier methods: FinDPO, Fin-R1, and LLMs Meet Finance | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimization target** | Stock-price reaction or volatility-adjusted market labels. [3] [7] | Synthetic sentiment preferences, answer correctness, format, or output length. [8] [6] [5] | Do not treat all "RL" or "preference" gains as the same kind of evidence. |
| **Trading scope** | Trading-R1 reports a held-out backtest; FinGPT reports no trading P&L. [3] [7] | FinDPO reports a portfolio simulation; Fin-R1 and LLMs Meet Finance report no trading P&L. [8] [6] [5] | Only FinDPO and Trading-R1 supply direct trading-result evidence in the reviewed set. |
| **Baseline quality** | Trading-R1 names many baselines, but the visible official table lacks paired baseline metrics and costs. [7] [7] | FinDPO lists multiple sentiment baselines but does not report FinSFT in the transaction-cost portfolio table; reasoning papers often report benchmark gains without trading baselines. [8] [5] | A named baseline is not enough; require paired numbers, identical costs, and identical windows. |
| **Test-window disclosure** | Trading-R1 states June 1-August 31, 2024 as held out. [7] | FinDPO states February 2015-June 2021, but day-level dates are NOT REPORTED; Fin-R1 and LLMs Meet Finance do not report trading windows. [8] [6] [5] | Month-only windows are insufficient for a precise replication claim. |
| **Transaction-cost disclosure** | Trading-R1: NOT REPORTED. [7] | FinDPO explicitly tests 1-5 bps and reports 5 bps results; finance-reasoning papers: NOT REPORTED. [8] [6] [5] | Cost sensitivity is the strongest visible threat to headline returns. |
| **Failure evidence** | FinGPT lacks a P&L test; Trading-R1 lacks complete cost and paired-baseline disclosure. [3] [7] | FinDPO warns about frictionless optimism; LLMs Meet Finance shows DPO degradation on NER and benchmark-only RL gains. [8] [5] | The defensible conclusion is conditional promise, not general superiority. |

| Bottom line | Evidence-based conclusion | Source URL |
|---|---|---|
| **Does preference tuning beat prompting or SFT for trading decisions?** | **Not established generally.** FinDPO is a promising DPO portfolio case, but its strongest table does not provide a clean DPO-versus-SFT P&L comparison. FinGPT and LLMs Meet Finance show task-level gains without trading P&L. Trading-R1 reports RFT trading numbers but does not disclose transaction costs or complete paired baseline metrics in the cited official table. [8] [3] [5] [7] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |
| **How well do realised-market rewards work?** | They can produce a sentiment-classification gain in FinGPT and a positive held-out trading result in Trading-R1, but FinGPT has no P&L evaluation and Trading-R1 has incomplete cost and baseline disclosure. [3] [7] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11420 |
| **What is the main reported failure mode?** | Transaction-cost sensitivity and frictionless-backtest optimism are explicitly reported for FinDPO; DPO also degrades NER F1 in LLMs Meet Finance. Quantified reward hacking and overfitting rates are NOT REPORTED in the reviewed finance preference papers. [8] [5] | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18417 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13125 |
