# Evidence-Based Architecture Choice for Daily Equity Trading

## Executive Insights

- **Bottom Line**: No published study located matches all of the requested constraints at once: five liquid US large-cap equities, daily bars, free data, transaction costs, buy-and-hold, and the S&P 500. The closest direct comparison favors the hybrid, but it uses six stocks, monthly strategy generation, no reported trading costs, and no buy-and-hold benchmark [18]. -> Treat the result as a research lead, not production evidence.
- **Direct Head-to-Head**: The strongest A/B/D comparison reports mean Sharpe ratios of **1.10 for LLM plus RL, 0.64 for RL-only, and 1.03 for LLM-only** on six large-cap stocks [18]. -> D is the evidence leader narrowly, with low-to-moderate confidence.
- **Pure RL**: FinRL's daily Dow 30 experiment reports a PPO/A2C/DDPG ensemble Sharpe of **2.81**, versus **2.02** for the DJIA and **1.98** for a minimum-variance benchmark, but omits equity transaction costs and equity buy-and-hold [5]. -> A is the safest control baseline, not proof of net alpha.
- **LLM Agents**: FinMem is unusually close to the target because it uses daily decisions on five stocks and compares Buy-and-Hold, DRL, and LLM agents; it reports that all tested memory settings beat Buy-and-Hold, but omits transaction costs and accessible results do not expose complete numerical tables [16]. -> B is promising but under-audited.
- **Preference Tuning**: FinDPO reports **458.97% cumulative return, 66.64% annualized return, and Sharpe 2.03 at 5 bps** on a daily S&P 500 long-short portfolio, against an S&P benchmark with **83.12% cumulative return and Sharpe 0.62** [20]. It is a sentiment model feeding a separate portfolio constructor, not an end-to-end trading controller [20]. -> C has signal evidence, not control-architecture evidence.
- **LLM Leakage**: Profit Mirage compares historical and post-knowledge-window periods and reports Sharpe decay of **51.48% to 62.23%** and total-return decay of **50.18% to 71.85%** across financial agents [27]. -> Any LLM layer needs temporal masking, point-in-time data, and a contamination audit.
- **Statistical Validity**: The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for non-normality, sample length, dispersion across trials, and the number of independent trials [11]. LLM strategy loops create dozens of implicit backtests, while recent leakage-safe work argues that DSR, PBO, and strict point-in-time protocols are rarely applied [21]. -> Log every trial and deflate the selected result.
- **Recommendation**: Rank D first for published comparative evidence, A second for a deployable baseline, B third, and C fourth. Build A first, run D as a pre-registered shadow arm, and do not let B or C control capital without the same costed, leakage-safe test.

## Decision Standard and Evidence Gaps

The relevant question is not which paper reports the largest return. It is which architecture survives a matched evaluation. For every result below, the comparison records the universe, dates, decision frequency, cost treatment, baseline, and whether the reported strategy beats an explicitly defined buy-and-hold portfolio. A reported Sharpe without those fields is a research observation, not an engineering acceptance test.

The evidence base has a structural mismatch with the requested system. FinRL supplies a public DRL implementation and a daily US equity experiment, but its equity result does not specify transaction costs and does not report equity buy-and-hold. FinMem supplies the closest five-stock daily head-to-head, but its tables and cost model are incomplete. The hybrid paper supplies the cleanest cross-architecture comparison, but it uses six stocks and monthly policy generation. FinDPO supplies a cost sensitivity analysis, but it is a sentiment-to-portfolio pipeline rather than a learned execution controller.

There is therefore no direct study that compares A, B, C, and D on the same five daily US equities with free, point-in-time data, realistic costs, buy-and-hold, and the S&P 500. This is a material negative finding. It means the requested choice must be made by weighting directness, methodological controls, and operational simplicity rather than by pooling headline returns from incomparable papers.

## A. Pure Deep RL: Broadest Engineering Baseline, Weak Net-Performance Proof

FinRL is the clearest public reference point for A. Its equity experiment trains on Dow 30 constituents from 2009-01-01 through 2020-06-30 and tests from 2020-07-01 through 2021-06-30 using daily data. The ensemble combines PPO, A2C, and DDPG; its reported annual return is 52.61% and Sharpe is 2.81, compared with Sharpe 2.02 for the DJIA and 1.98 for minimum variance [5]. This is useful evidence that a conventional DRL environment can produce a functioning policy and that PPO/A2C/DDPG are implementable with public code.

The result does not establish that the policy beats investable passive alternatives after frictions. The equity experiment does not specify transaction costs, slippage, spread, turnover penalties, or borrowing constraints, and it does not report an equity buy-and-hold outcome [5]. The same paper's cryptocurrency experiment does include transaction costs and compares a PPO portfolio with equal weight and Bitcoin buy-and-hold, but it uses ten cryptocurrencies at five-minute frequency from 2021-10-21 to 2021-10-30, so it cannot validate a daily five-equity system [5]. Mixing the two experiments would be an asset-class and frequency error.

A therefore earns second place in the evidence ranking but first place in the implementation sequence. It has the lowest architectural complexity, the most transparent control loop, and the cleanest path to a free-data reproducibility package. Its correct acceptance test should be stricter than FinRL's: walk-forward splits, point-in-time universe membership, explicit spread and commission assumptions, turnover reporting, and a five-stock equal-weight buy-and-hold portfolio alongside the S&P 500. The mechanism is straightforward: RL learns a policy over prices, positions, cash, and costs; the implication is that any apparent edge can be stress-tested without an opaque language-model inference layer.

## B. LLM-Agent Trading: Direct Daily Evidence Exists, but Costs and Leakage Dominate

FinMem is important because it compares unlike controllers in one daily experiment. The system makes Buy, Sell, or Hold decisions for one share of a stock. The study identifies five stocks and uses training dates from 17 August 2021 to 5 October 2022 and testing dates from 6 October 2022 to 10 April 2023 [16]. It compares Buy-and-Hold, DRL agents including PPO and DQN, and two LLM-based agents [16]. The paper reports that every tested memory-retrieval setting beats Buy-and-Hold, that some settings have Sharpe ratios above 2, and that K=5 has the lowest volatility and maximum drawdown [16].

That is the strongest B result for target similarity, but it is not a strong net-of-cost result. Transaction costs are not reported, complete numerical tables are not available in the accessible evidence, and the paper does not establish that its five-stock sample is the same liquid large-cap universe an engineer would choose today. An agent that changes position daily can lose its apparent edge when spread, slippage, and tax-lot effects are added. The correct interpretation is that memory and textual reasoning may help action selection in a short historical window, not that an LLM agent has demonstrated scalable alpha.

TradingAgents, submitted 28 December 2024, uses specialized fundamental, sentiment, technical, trader, and risk agents. Its historical simulation uses AAPL, GOOGL, and AMZN from 1 January through 29 March 2024, and compares the agent with equal-weight Buy-and-Hold plus MACD, KDJ+RSI, ZMR, and SMA rules [24][4]. The paper reports superior cumulative return, Sharpe, and drawdown relative to its baselines, but the reported design does not include commissions, spreads, slippage, or borrowing costs [24][4]. It is not a direct comparison with deep RL.

StockBench, submitted 2 October 2025 and revised 2 March 2026, is a useful counterweight. It evaluates daily agents on the top 20 DJIA stocks over 3 March to 30 June 2025, or 82 trading days. Each run starts with $100,000, the equal-weight Buy-and-Hold portfolio returns 0.4%, and most tested models beat that baseline while having lower drawdowns; however, the benchmark models trading costs, slippage, and liquidity limits as absent [25][28]. The mechanism behind the strong results may therefore be unrestricted rebalancing in a short favorable window. B is third: its narrative capability is real, but its evidence is less robust than its headline returns imply.

## C. RLHF, DPO, and Preference-Tuned Finance LLMs: Strong Signal Study, Wrong Control Abstraction

FinGPT, submitted 9 June 2023, is an open-source finance LLM framework rather than a completed evidence package for daily equity control [23]. The distinction matters. RLHF or DPO can make a model follow preferred sentiment labels, risk preferences, or explanations, but preference alignment is not the same as learning a sequential policy under transaction costs. No located FinGPT result provides a matched end-to-end A/B/C/D trading experiment with the requested universe, costs, buy-and-hold, and S&P 500.

FinDPO, submitted 24 July 2025, is the strongest C-style result. It uses Financial PhraseBank, Twitter Financial News, and GPT-labeled financial news to train an LLM with pairwise preferences derived from sentiment labels. In the portfolio experiment, daily news and returns cover February 2015 through June 2021, assets are ranked by continuous sentiment, and the strategy rebalances a long-short portfolio daily [20]. The reported 5 bps result is 458.97% cumulative, 66.64% annualized, Sharpe 2.03, Sortino 3.75, and Calmar 2.21; the S&P benchmark is 83.12% cumulative, 11.34% annualized, Sharpe 0.62, Sortino 0.81, and Calmar 0.41 [20]. On the paper's benchmark definition, FinDPO wins by a large margin and remains positive after 5 bps.

There are three reasons not to promote that result to architecture C. First, FinDPO is not an end-to-end trading policy: it produces sentiment scores and a separate portfolio-construction framework turns them into positions [20]. Second, the paper does not provide separate portfolio train and test dates and does not call the S&P benchmark buy-and-hold [20]. Third, the sample construction excludes firms without sufficient news and excludes FinBERT from one evaluation because of data leakage, showing that data curation is central to the result [20]. C is therefore fourth as a control architecture, although its sentiment module could be a carefully gated feature for A or D.

## D. Hybrid LLM-Shaped RL: Best Direct Comparison, Still a Research Hypothesis

The 25 October 2025 LLM Guided Reinforcement Learning paper is the only located study with a meaningful three-way head-to-head among an LLM-only strategy, an RL-only policy, and an LLM plus RL hybrid. It uses AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, META, MSFT, and TSLA, generates strategies monthly every 20 trading days, and backtests 2018-2020. The hybrid uses GPT-4o Mini Strategist and Analyst agents to produce high-level policies and signals while leaving the DDQN tactical executor unchanged [18].

The reported mean Sharpe is 1.10 for LLM plus RL, 0.64 for RL-only, and 1.03 for LLM-only. Asset-level values are supplied for all six assets, and the reported mean maximum drawdowns are 0.31, 0.36, and 0.35 respectively [18]. This is the strongest positive evidence for D because the same environment and six-stock test are used rather than comparing unrelated papers. It supports a plausible mechanism: the slow LLM layer supplies regime or news context, while DDQN handles tactical execution and avoids asking a language model to manage every order.

The paper also contains reasons to discount the result. It reports API and development costs, but not transaction commissions, spread, slippage, market impact, or borrow costs [18]. It does not report buy-and-hold or S&P 500. The test universe has six names rather than five, and monthly strategy generation is not daily-bar policy control. The paper says the hybrid beats RL-only in four of six assets, while the displayed Sharpe values are higher for the hybrid than RL-only on each listed asset; that internal tension should be resolved from code before relying on the result [18]. The paper also states that the selected universe's positive drift discouraged sustained short positions and that reward shaping remains necessary [18].

Finally, D in the question specifies an LLM plus knowledge-graph layer that reshapes observations, features, or reward. The published hybrid describes Strategist and Analyst agents and an unchanged DDQN; it does not demonstrate a knowledge graph or a causal reward-shaping ablation [18]. D is therefore the most promising architecture to test, not a validated design. It should enter the engineering program as a slow feature or regime overlay with a frozen, auditable interface, never as an unconstrained reward writer.

## Failure Literature: Why Headline Returns Are Not Enough

Backtest overfitting is the first failure mode. The Probability of Backtest Overfitting paper, revised February 2015, proposes combinatorially symmetric cross-validation because ordinary holdout methods can be unreliable for investment simulations [15][8]. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio paper, first version April 2014 and revised 31 July 2014, explains that the best Sharpe among many trials rises even when all strategies are random. DSR adjusts for sample length, skewness, kurtosis, the variance of tested Sharpe ratios, and the number of independent trials [11]. Every RL hyperparameter sweep and every LLM prompt or debate round expands that trial count.

LLM systems make selection bias unusually easy to hide. A strategy-discovery loop may propose, backtest, critique, and revise dozens of strategies before a human sees one reported result [21]. The 27 August 2026 leakage-safe study responds with registry-validated tools, server-side rejection of look-ahead features, deterministic point-in-time execution, realistic costs, a held-out evaluation window, and a complete trial ledger [21]. Its engineering lesson is more important than any single return: the experiment must record failed candidates as well as the selected candidate, and the selection threshold must be corrected for all trials.

LLM pretraining leakage is a second, architecture-specific threat. The September 2023 GPT study uses S&P 500 headlines, an in-sample period from January 2015 through September 2021, and an out-of-sample period from October 2021 through December 2022. It compares original headlines with anonymized versions and runs long-only, short-only, and long-short daily portfolios [1]. It omits transaction costs and explicitly says the strategies are not intended to be feasible [1]. The authors find that a negative distraction effect outweighs any positive look-ahead benefit and that GPT-3.5 does not exploit look-ahead well, but this does not make ordinary backtests safe [1].

Profit Mirage, submitted 9 October 2025, provides the stronger warning. It compares NASDAQ-100 stocks in Q2-Q3 2021 with Q3-Q4 2024, when market returns were similar at 13.79% and 13.35%, and reports that five financial agents all suffer post-release decay. Sharpe decay ranges from 51.48% to 62.23%, total-return decay from 50.18% to 71.85%, and TradingAgents suffers 55.68% Sharpe decay [27]. Counterfactual tests found that the worst model left 82.13% of predictions unchanged after major inputs were altered, while GPT-4o and peer models answered more than 85% of 2,000 historical financial questions correctly despite the benchmark period overlapping contemporary training cutoffs [27]. This is direct evidence that a language model can recite historical patterns rather than forecast tradable information.

Irreproducibility is a third failure mode. A 2024 Review of Financial Studies experiment ran more than 1,000 empirical answers from 168 finance research teams using the same raw data and regenerated the exact original result only 52% of the time [29]. That study is not a failed replication of FinRL or TradingAgents, and no located independent replication of the four architecture classes met the exact five-stock daily specification. It nevertheless sets the appropriate prior: public code and a paper are not enough. The acceptance package should include pinned data snapshots, timestamped features, seeds, all failed trials, exact cost assumptions, and an independent rerun.

## Synthesis and Ranked Recommendation

The architectures differ along four dimensions. A learns a fast sequential policy directly from numeric state and has the lowest inference and audit burden, but published equity tests often omit costs or buy-and-hold. B has the richest contextual interface and can debate news and fundamentals, but its decision loop is expensive, nondeterministic, and vulnerable to memorization; StockBench and Profit Mirage show that most or many headline wins are not robust. C improves preference following or sentiment ranking, but the strongest result is a separate signal pipeline, not a controller that learns inventory, turnover, and execution. D separates slow semantic context from fast control and wins the only clean three-way comparison, but its evidence is one out-of-spec study with missing frictions and no S&P benchmark.

| Rank | Architecture | Confidence in published evidence for the requested system | Recommendation |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **D. Hybrid LLM-shaped RL** | **Low to moderate** | Best research candidate because of the direct mean-Sharpe comparison. Use only as a slow, frozen feature or regime overlay; do not allow free-form reward rewriting. |
| **2** | **A. Pure deep RL** | **Moderate for a baseline, low for net alpha** | Build first as the production control and benchmark. Require costed walk-forward validation against five-stock Buy-and-Hold and the S&P 500. |
| **3** | **B. LLM-agent trading** | **Low** | Use for offline feature generation or analyst proposals, not direct capital control, until contamination and cost tests pass. |
| **4** | **C. RLHF/DPO finance LLM** | **Very low for end-to-end control; moderate for sentiment features** | Treat FinDPO-like output as an exogenous signal with strict timestamping, not as the trading policy. |

The single strongest evidence for selecting D is the matched 2025 comparison: hybrid mean Sharpe 1.10 versus 0.64 for RL-only and 1.03 for LLM-only [18]. The single strongest evidence against selecting D as production control is that the same study omits trading costs and buy-and-hold, uses monthly strategy generation, and does not implement the requested knowledge graph [18]. The correct engineering decision is consequently staged: implement A with transparent costs and fixed data first; add D as a preregistered shadow model that can modify only auditable observations or a bounded regime feature; compare both against the same five-stock Buy-and-Hold portfolio and S&P 500; and promote D only if its advantage survives DSR/PBO correction, leakage audits, and an untouched post-selection window.

This staged choice also resolves the apparent ranking conflict. D ranks first for comparative research evidence, while A is the first system to build because its failure surface is smaller and its performance can be falsified more cheaply. If D cannot beat A after costs and trial correction, the LLM layer has not earned control authority. If it does, the result will be materially stronger than any current paper because it will finally satisfy the missing five-stock, daily, free-data, costed, Buy-and-Hold, and S&P 500 comparison.
